# Vanity word library

The built-in dictionary combines the original vanity vocabulary with curated degen slang, crypto social shorthand, trading terms, DeFi vocabulary, blockchain infrastructure, NFT culture, meme names, political vocabulary, and Jewish cultural and religious terms. Political entries cover civic institutions, elections, ideologies, and public figures. Jewish entries include holidays, rituals, texts, food, Hebrew and Yiddish vocabulary, and common transliteration variants. It is a search vocabulary, not an endorsement of tokens, projects, people, or trading strategies.

The additions live in `src/word-library.js`, grouped for maintenance. `src/matching.js` merges them with the existing list and removes duplicates. Words are lowercase ASCII letters and digits. Multiword expressions use joined spelling, such as `diamondhands`, `exitliquidity`, and `bondingcurve`, because wallet addresses cannot contain spaces. Hebrew and Yiddish entries use Latin transliterations to fit address alphabets. Spelling variants are explicit entries; look-alike digit matching remains handled by the matcher.

Reference checks used [BabyPips' crypto slang index](https://www.babypips.com/crypto/glossary/topics/slang) for established shorthand and [Meme Central's memecoin glossary](https://memecentral.fun/guides/memecoin-glossary) for launch mechanics and trader slang. These sources were checked on October 29, 2026. Entries are a curated vocabulary assembled with broader crypto terminology, not a copied glossary, a live popularity ranking, or a claim that every item is currently trending. No source definitions or token descriptions are reproduced.

The UI reports the number of dictionary words eligible for the current chain alphabet, look-alike setting, minimum word length, and search zone. First 4, Last 4, and combined First 4 + Last 4 searches allow words up to four characters long; Last 5 allows five and Last 6 allows six. The combined option checks each end independently. Choose Anywhere for longer words. Two-letter shorthand is retained in the library but requires choosing a two-character minimum. Longer terms remain subject to the selected chain's address length and alphabet. Broader dictionaries make word hits more common; the existing rarity tiers remain aesthetic pattern rules.

Common English uses the 2,048-word BIP39 English list already provided by `@scure/bip39` (MIT). It is a curated familiar-word set, not a measured frequency ranking. Using these public words for address matching does not select, constrain, or otherwise influence generated recovery phrases. The crypto/vanity, political, and Jewish vocabularies are separate selectable packs; duplicates across packs are removed.

Expanded English is a filtered, locally bundled edition of an-array-of-english-words 2.0.0. Its lowercase entries load on demand. The original MIT notice is included in [the dataset licenses](licenses/an-array-of-english-words.txt).

## Broad English and slang

Broad English and Slang / abbreviations / typos are enabled by default. Overlapping entries are deduplicated when multiple packs are selected. The public build includes its word lists and works without manual dictionary setup.

Broad English merges all eligible entries from [dwyl/english-words, words_alpha.txt](https://github.com/dwyl/english-words) (Unlicense / public domain; retrieved October 30, 2026) and `an-array-of-english-words` 2.0.0 (MIT). This includes uncommon words, inflections, and variant spellings, not just familiar modern English. Source licenses are copied to `docs/licenses/dwyl-english-words.txt` and `docs/licenses/an-array-of-english-words.txt`.

The slang pack is a project-curated supplemental vocabulary covering crypto shorthand, internet slang, gaming terms, text-message abbreviations, phonetic spellings, and common typo variants. It does not exhaust every regional expression or possible misspelling. Entries do not gain a desirability bonus simply for belonging to these new packs. A larger vocabulary makes matches more frequent and adds matching work; it does not make every match rarer. Existing rarity thresholds remain unchanged.

## Countries, cities, companies, and public figures

Four selectable packs are enabled by default and available as result-category buttons: Countries (303 normalized names/aliases), Major cities (6,391), Companies (1,397), and Public figures (1,658). These packs allow 2–44 lowercase ASCII letters/digits, not just 4–6 characters. Spaces, punctuation, and accents are removed for address matching; for example, New York City becomes newyorkcity and Elon Musk becomes elonmusk. Named aliases are also included. Dictionary scope and minimum length still apply; seven-character words such as america need Anywhere or an explicit end filter. Two-character abbreviations require a two-character dictionary minimum. Entity entries do not receive an automatic desirability bonus. Rarity tiers remain unchanged.

Countries includes every English name variant from the 250 country/territory records in the English list of i18n-iso-countries, retrieved October 30, 2026, plus explicit aliases such as america, usa, britain, and uae. This includes dependent territories and the source list’s Kosovo entry, and is not a statement about diplomatic recognition. Source: https://github.com/michaelwittig/node-i18n-iso-countries/blob/master/langs/en.json. Copyright 2016 widdix GmbH, MIT; license copied to docs/licenses/i18n-iso-countries.txt. The source snapshot is src/data/country-names-source.json. Transformations: English aliases flattened, lowercase ASCII normalization, punctuation/space removal, length filtering, and deduplication.

Major cities derives from GeoNames cities15000.zip, retrieved October 30, 2026. It selects 6,364 records whose reported population is at least 100,000 or whose feature code is PPLC (capital), takes the primary and ASCII name fields, and adds curated city aliases and notable smaller destinations. These source population values are not a current census or ranking. Attribution: GeoNames, https://www.geonames.org/; source https://download.geonames.org/export/dump/cities15000.zip; Creative Commons Attribution 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. Full license copied to docs/licenses/geonames-cc-by-4.0.txt. Transformations: selection, normalization, deduplication, and supplemental aliases. Dataset coverage and population estimates are inherited from GeoNames; the pack is not every town in the world.

Companies and Public figures are project-curated vocabularies spanning technology, finance, crypto, consumer brands, industry, entrepreneurs, investors, creators, streamers, sports, entertainment, and political commentators. They include historic and current names, full joined names, recognizable surnames, public handles, and selected aliases. They are not exhaustive lists of every company or influential person, a live prominence ranking, an assertion of current employment, or endorsements. Names can overlap other word categories. All packs are shipped locally; no lookup occurs during wallet generation.

The entity packs also participate in independent rare-find overflow. Saved archives reclassify existing matched-word metadata when dictionary membership changes, preserving keys, scores, and batch ownership. Reclassification does not rescan previously saved addresses for words that were not recorded as matches. The GPU dictionary limit is 400,000 entries so enabling every pack, including Expanded English, remains supported.

## Pokémon

The Pokémon pack is enabled by default and has a Pokémon result-category button in the hunt and saved collection pages. It includes all 1,025 species and all 1,351 species/form entries returned by PokéAPI on October 30, 2026, yielding 1,390 unique normalized terms including explicit nidoranfemale and nidoranmale aliases. Sources: https://pokeapi.co/api/v2/pokemon-species?limit=2000 and https://pokeapi.co/api/v2/pokemon?limit=10000. Raw snapshots are stored in src/data/pokemon-species-source.json and src/data/pokemon-forms-source.json; the matching pack is src/data/entity-pokemon.json. Coverage reflects these source snapshots, including alternate forms listed by the API, rather than a promise to include future releases. Pokémon names belong to their respective owners; this project is unaffiliated.

Names are lowercase with punctuation and separators removed: Mr. Mime becomes mrmime, Type: Null becomes typenull, and Charizard Mega X becomes charizardmegax. Existing case-insensitive and digit-alias matching applies. Names longer than the selected search zone require Anywhere or an explicit start/end filter. The pack also participates in rare-find overflow, uses the existing rarity scoring, and requires no network requests during hunts.

## Sports, entertainment, music, and collecting

The expansion in src/theme-words.js adds 36 selectable categories with 6,195 distinct terms (6,475 category memberships before cross-category deduplication). These are project-curated names, established aliases, and subject vocabulary, including historic and contemporary entries; they are not exhaustive directories or continuously updated popularity rankings. Names are normalized into lowercase address-safe terms. Multiword names use joined forms, such as ellalangley, charlottedewitte, worldcup2026, and ganjawhitenight. Short aliases and abbreviations are included where useful; categories can overlap. Every pack is enabled by default, can be disabled independently, and persists its selection. All packs participate in independent rare discovery. Existing score thresholds and desirability bonuses are unchanged; category membership does not make a wallet rarer.

The categories cover sports and leagues; teams; athletes; motorsport; combat sports and wrestling; sporting events; cultural and technology events; holidays; trading cards and grading; designer toys and figures; sneakers and streetwear; watches and jewelry; coins, art, and memorabilia; comics; anime; gaming; esports; pop and R&B; hip-hop; rock and metal; country and folk; Latin, Afrobeats, and global music; K-pop and J-pop; general electronic music; house; techno and hard dance; trance and psytrance; dubstep and bass; drum & bass and jungle; music festivals and clubs; music equipment and vinyl culture; film and television; cars; space and science; food and drinks; and fantasy, mythology, and tabletop games.

Current-source checks on October 30, 2026 included Billboard Artist 100 (https://www.billboard.com/charts/artist-100/), whose retrieved artist headings included Morgan Wallen, Olivia Rodrigo, Zach Bryan, Ella Langley, Taylor Swift, Drake, Bruno Mars, Michael Jackson, Olivia Dean, and Tate McRae. These names are included without claiming a position or copying chart descriptions. FIFA's 2026 World Cup page was also accessible (https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026). DJ Mag's Top 100 DJs page (https://djmag.com/top100djs) returned HTTP 403; electronic artist inclusion is editorial and is not attributed to a verified current DJ Mag ranking. Collectibles, sports, and the broader music library are curated coverage rather than a measured trend leaderboard. No network lookups run during wallet generation.

Result categories remain clickable buttons in both pages. Their area and the word-pack list have bounded heights to preserve room for results. Short search zones still restrict the eligible word lengths: a six-character suffix cannot contain the full name charlottedewitte. Use Anywhere or an explicit start/end filter for longer names. Adding dictionaries increases matching work, so disable unwanted primary packs when focusing a hunt; rare discovery retains its independent vocabulary.

## Historical people

The curated historical expansion in src/historical-words.js adds eight packs containing 3,506 distinct normalized names and aliases: Historical rulers & political leaders; Historical military leaders; Historical inventors & scientists; Historical artists & architects; Historical writers & philosophers; Historical reformers & spiritual figures; Historical explorers & pioneers; and Historical dictators, outlaws & notorious figures. Coverage spans ancient, medieval, early modern, and modern figures across multiple regions, with some contemporary figures and traditional or partly legendary figures included where culturally prominent. It is broad editorial coverage, not an exhaustive historical register or a popularity ranking. Group membership indicates a role or historical reputation, not endorsement or a uniform moral judgment.

Full joined names and explicitly chosen shorter names, surnames, titles, and transliteration variants are included, with overlap allowed between categories. Short names may have unrelated meanings or identify several people; these are address-matching tokens rather than unique person identifiers. The packs use the existing case-insensitive/look-alike matching and rarity rules, appear as search checkboxes and result-category buttons, persist their selections, and participate in independent rare discovery. Existing saved hit tags are reclassified when the backend loads the updated dictionary; previously discarded addresses are not recovered or rescanned. Longer names need Anywhere or an explicit start/end filter.

Given-name aliases are also added from a curated recognized-name list when the category contains a joined full name and the remaining surname or name alias exists in the historical vocabulary. For example, adolf, hitler, and adolfhitler are independently searchable, as are nikola, tesla, and nikolatesla. This avoids treating arbitrary slices of names as given names. The earlier 3,506-term count describes the initial historical expansion before this addition. Short names are scored for their own matching difficulty; they do not inherit the full name's score or rarity. Search-zone length and minimum-word-length settings still apply.

## Direct-word hunts

The main page's Direct words field accepts comma- or space-separated alternatives, such as example, search, words. When populated, it replaces the primary dictionary with those words and accepts a match to any one of them in the selected search zone, regardless of the hunt minimum rarity or dictionary minimum length. Typed terms must contain 2–44 letters or digits. With Fit typed words enabled, a term longer than a single-end zone uses its first N characters for a First N zone or last N characters for a Last N zone. The UI displays the effective search fragments; those fragments must be representable in the selected network. Anywhere retains the full word, and the joined-end mode still requires exactly eight characters. Case-insensitive and optional look-alike matching still apply. Starts with, Ends with, Required repeat, and Ignore repeated remain additional constraints. A repeated run alone does not count as a direct-word match. Actual scores and rarity labels are unchanged.

Rare-find overflow continues to scan the full address against its independent dictionary and rarity threshold. Its capacity and stopping behavior remain unchanged. Clear Direct words to restore the selected word packs and custom dictionary. Existing results-view filters still control what is visible, and the collection target is shared across the direct-word alternatives rather than a quota per word.

The Search zone option **First 4 + last 4 · ONE 8-character word** joins the first four and last four address characters, excluding network prefixes, and requires a complete eight-character dictionary or Direct words match. For example, `dead…beef` matches `deadbeef`. Shorter words and repeats alone do not qualify. The two ends are highlighted separately but scored together as one word; case and look-alike matching, rarity, explicit end constraints, and repeat filters still apply. Rare finds continue to scan independently.

Scoring version 3 rewards the casing of actual Solana word matches: ALL CAPS adds 18 points, Proper Case adds 9, and lowercase or scattered case adds zero. Exact spelling retains its 6-point readability bonus; look-alike substitutions retain the 3-point penalty per substitution and receive no casing bonus. Thus the same word in the same search zone ranks `TEST` above `Test`, then `test`/`tEsT`, then `TES7`. Joined eight-character words are judged as one combined word. Solana letter repeats also receive the casing bonus. Ethereum checksum case and Bitcoin presentation case receive no casing bonus. Existing saved scores are retained; the new scoring applies to newly evaluated matches.

Rare-find overflow has independent controls for word source, minimum word length (2–12), search zone, look-alike matching, and excluding repeat runs of three or more characters. Built-in core preserves the original independent curated dictionary (including common English and entity/theme packs); it does not mirror library checkboxes or include every expanded English term. Selected library + custom words uses the enabled packs and optional custom words, while Current search words also honors Direct words replacing that list. Overflow words only uses its own comma/space-separated input. The displayed count includes only words that fit the overflow zone, minimum length, and network. The overflow rarity threshold always applies, even when using Direct words as its source. Minimum length restricts words, not repeat runs; repeated patterns can still qualify unless excluded. These settings persist and leave the primary search filters independent.

Supplemental vocabulary expands 55 category packs with 7–10-character words and names; complete Pokémon and expanded-English datasets are retained. First 5–10 and Last 7–10 zones complement the existing end zones, and the main minimum word length now supports up to 10. Dictionary entries remain full words; fitting applies to typed Direct words, optional custom words, and explicit Starts with/Ends with terms. For example, `whalepool` with Last 6 becomes `lepool`; with First 4 it becomes `whal`. Highlight labels and scores use the actual matched fragment. Disable Fit typed words to require the original term. The two separate four-character zones do not automatically split a long Direct word; use the joined-eight mode for one eight-character word across both ends.

Single result stops after one new primary match on each run, without clearing previously collected wallets. Rare finds remain independent and their capacity can still stop a hunt. Fill continues until its collection target; Keep the best continues replacing weaker matches. The visual Match probability bar sits beneath Current search and before the counters. It shows the estimated chance across the current run, per-address odds, search matches found this run, and checks or remaining time to a selected 50%, 95%, or 99% chance. It resets for a new hunt or changed search settings. A full-looking bar is not a guarantee of a match. It uses a uniform independent-character model and optimistic union bounds for multiple words or positions, before rarity/repeat restrictions; it does not count repeat-only finds or promise a completion time. Both-end joint-word estimates are currently unavailable rather than shown as falsely precise values.
